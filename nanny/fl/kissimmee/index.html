What a nanny does

  • Caring for your children in your home on a regular schedule
  • Meals, naps, bathing and daily routines
  • School and activity pick-ups (agree on driving and car seats)
  • Play, homework help and age-appropriate activities
  • Light housekeeping related to the children

What to ask before you hire

  • Experience with children the same age as yours
  • CPR and first-aid certification
  • How they handle discipline, screens and naps
  • References from families they worked for
  • Whether they can drive and have a clean driving record, if needed
  • If you want a bilingual nanny: which language they'll speak with your children

Hiring legally in Florida

A nanny who works in your home on your schedule is your household employee. In 2026, if you pay them $3,000 or more in cash wages in the year, you owe Social Security and Medicare taxes and file Schedule H with your tax return (IRS Publication 926). Florida has no state income tax; Florida reemployment tax applies if you pay $1,000 or more in a calendar quarter. See our guide: how to hire a household employee in Florida.

What's the difference between a nanny and a babysitter?

A nanny works a regular, ongoing schedule (often full-time) and becomes part of your routine. A babysitter is usually occasional: evenings, weekends or a few hours.
