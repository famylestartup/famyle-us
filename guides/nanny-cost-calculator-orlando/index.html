Nanny Cost Calculator: Orlando, FL Weekly Pay, Overtime and Taxes (2026)

Calculate a nanny's weekly and yearly cost in Orlando, including overtime and the employer taxes a Florida household pays in 2026.

Updated November 19, 2026

Quick answer: A full-time nanny in Orlando, FL (40 hours a week) at the typical $20.79–$26.36 per hour (Care.com, November 2026) costs about $832–$1,054 a week, or $43,243–$54,829 a year in pay. As a household employer you add about $3,539–$4,425 a year in taxes: 7.65% Social Security and Medicare once you pay $3,000 in 2026, plus federal and Florida unemployment tax on the first $7,000. A nanny who doesn't live with you gets 1.5x pay after 40 hours a week.

How to hire, step by step

  1. Decide the schedule: hours a week, live-in or live-out, and whether driving is part of the job.
  2. Set the pay with the calculator; budget the employer taxes as well.
  3. Post your job for free with your ZIP code, schedule and pay. Nannies near you apply.
  4. Interview and call references from other families. A paid trial day shows how the nanny and your kids get along.
  5. Put it in writing: hours, pay, overtime, paid time off, duties and notice period.
  6. Set up payroll: get an EIN, complete Form I-9 and plan for Schedule H and Form W-2. See nanny taxes in Florida.

Red flags

  • A request to be paid “off the books” when you will pay $3,000 or more in the year: the tax obligation stays with you as the employer.
  • No references you can actually call.
  • Unwillingness to agree on hours and duties in writing.
  • Pay below the Florida minimum wage for an employee.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a full-time nanny cost per year in Orlando?

About $43,243–$54,829 in pay for 40 hours a week at $20.79–$26.36 per hour (Care.com, November 2026), plus about $3,539–$4,425 in employer taxes.

Do nannies get overtime in Florida?

A household employee who does not live in your home must be paid 1.5 times the regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek under federal law. Live-in nannies are exempt from overtime but must still get at least minimum wage (U.S. Department of Labor).

What taxes do I pay for a nanny in Florida?

Social Security and Medicare (7.65% from you, 7.65% withheld from the nanny) once you pay $3,000 in 2026, plus federal unemployment tax and Florida reemployment tax on the first $7,000 if you pay $1,000 in a quarter. Florida has no state income tax (IRS Publication 926).

Do I have to withhold income tax from my nanny?

No. You withhold federal income tax only if the nanny asks and you agree (IRS Publication 926).
